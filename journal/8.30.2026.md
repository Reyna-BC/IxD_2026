## My Thoughts:
Reading this chapter was pretty interesting in terms of being able to look more into everyday objects in a much more in depth perspectives. 
For example, when he talks about the doors, I imagine the times I would mess up on how to open the door, whether by the design, the lack of visual aid like "PULL" or "PUSH", or by the design itself looking almost unique to me.
Something that I remembered taking my UI and UX Theory classes was that we mentioned about the differences between them, and one of the quotes that stood out to me on **page 4** of *"THE PSYCHOPATHOLOGY
OF EVERYDAY THINGS"* when making a design for anything was this;
"the products should also be delightful and enjoyable, which means that not only must the requirements of engineering, manufacturing, and ergonomics be satisfied, but attention must be paid to the entire experience, which
means the aesthetics of form and the quality of interaction." This is pretty much what I remember learning when it came to making designs of any kind, whether it is for games, applications for phones, furniture,
really anything that involves a user to interactive with. And it is still something I still do apply within my own projects as well, as building a stable foundation and trying to see improvements for a much more satisfying experience.
In a way, the chapter pretty much describes how I view when playing any sort of video games nowadays with level design, menus, interactions within the game itself, and navigation if I were to play if for the first time or so.
The journey of coming from someone who just only played games for a much more competitive scene than understanding it, was something I found a lot more interesting and enjoyable as I went further into the passion with more ambitions
to develop them than to just play. And while this chapter only had doors as being an example as to the mindset of what people who are engineers think like, as they are logical thinkers, it goes deeper on the psychological part of the human brain being a way more complexity than to just simply "Read the instructions.".
