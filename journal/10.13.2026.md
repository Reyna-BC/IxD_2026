## My Thoughts
Reading this chapter on *"THE PSYCHOPATHOLOGY
OF EVERYDAY THINGS"*, (chapter 4), was that I found it pretty interesting the quote that was said. "These four classes of constraints—physical, cultural,
semantic, and logical—seem to be universal, appearing in a wide variety of situations." This chapter talked about constraints within a design and having people figure out within
using those clues to understand what exactly they are doing within a design they are given with no such help or guidance other than to figure out with what they already know when it
comes to learning something new. The differences between these constraints are that 
*Physical* there is more limitations within it's own world, such as preventing wrong actions being done if it has been tried.
*Culture* constraint is more about social norms, yet not all of it is universally understood by everyone. Cultural behavior are represented in the mind by schemas, knowledge structures that contain the
general rules and information necessary for interpreting situations and for guiding behavior. 
*Semantics* is the study of meaning. Semantic constraints are those that rely upon the meaning of the situation to control the set of
possible actions.
*Logical* There are no physical or cultural principles but rather, there is a logical relationship between the spatial or
functional layout of components and the things that they affect or are affected by.
