## My Thoughts:
Reading this chapter was almost as similar as the first one, although it gets into more details about the process of design in seven steps. "The seven stages provide a guideline for developing new products or services. The gulfs are obvious places to start, for either gulf, whether of execution or evaluation, is an opportunity for product enhancement. The trick is to develop observational skills to detect them." page 43. I think another chunk of this chapter that I really liked was the question on, "Why do we need to know the human mind?" which is answered in the next sentence and it's because the things we create are usually used by humans, so you have to know what they think like. The mind is a very complicated organ that can't be simplified, as we have our own individual beliefs, behavior, and science. On page 45, the quote "Conscious attention is necessary to learn most things, but after the initial learning, continued practice and study, sometimes for thousands of hours over a period of years, produces what psychologists call “overlearning." Cognitive thoughts also lead to emotions as it is mentioned as well which is a very important factor on understanding humans when it comes to experience with what we design after all. If the design is causing frustration to the user, hard to understand or what to do, the results can lead it into not being very good, unless if that was your intention in the first place for it.
